## 1. Purpose
- The online storefront for home and kitchen products across the EU is slow on mobile, and conversion has dropped, which is reducing online revenue.
- Why now: the drop in conversion needs to be reversed quickly, so a full storefront and checkout redesign is planned within 3 months.

## 2. Objectives (SMART)
1. Improve mobile page load performance, measured by mobile page load time on key templates (home, category, product, checkout), achieving [target TBC] by end of Month 3.
2. Recover online conversion, measured by site-wide conversion rate (orders divided by sessions), achieving [target TBC] by [date TBC, suggested 4 to 8 weeks after go-live].
3. Reduce checkout drop-off, measured by checkout abandonment rate, achieving [target TBC] by [date TBC].
4. Launch the redesigned storefront and checkout in all current EU markets, measured by go-live completed with no critical defects open, by end of Month 3.
5. Maintain GDPR compliance at launch, measured by sign-off of cookie consent and customer data handling by the Data Protection Officer before go-live, by end of Month 3.

## 3. Scope
**In scope**
- Redesign of the storefront: home, category, search, product and content pages
- Redesign of the checkout journey, including basket and order confirmation
- Mobile-first design and front-end performance optimisation
- Cookie consent mechanism and customer data handling within the redesigned journeys
- Analytics and conversion tracking set-up to measure the objectives
- Testing (functional, performance, cross-device) and go-live

**Out of scope (proposed)**
- Replacement of the e-commerce platform (TBC)
- Changes to ERP, warehouse or fulfilment systems
- Changes to the product range, pricing or promotions strategy
- New marketing campaigns or new market launches
- Change of payment provider (TBC)

## 4. Stakeholders
**Primary**
- Project Sponsor: approves scope, budget and go-live
- Head of E-commerce: owns the storefront, conversion targets and trading impact
- Project Manager: delivers the project to scope, time and budget
- Data Protection Officer: confirms GDPR compliance before go-live

**Secondary**
- Marketing team: brand, content, campaigns and analytics
- Customer Service team: customer feedback and post-launch queries
- IT / Development team: build, integration and hosting
- Operations / Fulfilment team: confirms that orders flow correctly to back-office systems

**External**
- Customers in EU markets: usability, speed and trust at checkout
- Platform, hosting and agency suppliers (if used): delivery of build and infrastructure
- Payment provider: checkout integration and payment flows
- Data protection supervisory authorities: GDPR oversight

## 5. High-level requirements
- Fast, mobile-first storefront and checkout on common devices and browsers
- Simplified checkout journey with fewer steps and clear error handling
- Support for all current EU markets, languages and currencies (TBC)
- Cookie consent that lets customers accept, reject and manage preferences
- Customer data collected, stored and processed in line with the company's GDPR policies
- Conversion and performance tracking to measure the objectives
- Continuity of existing integrations (payments, orders, stock) at go-live

## 6. Constraints
- Timeframe: full redesign of storefront and checkout within 3 months
- Regulatory: the site must remain GDPR compliant, including cookie consent and customer data handling
- Live trading site: the redesign must not disrupt online sales during build and launch
- Budget: Not provided

## 7. Assumptions to validate
- The current e-commerce platform is retained and the redesign is front-end and journey focused (validate with: Head of E-commerce)
- Baseline figures for mobile speed and conversion are available from current analytics (validate with: Head of E-commerce)
- The internal team and/or supplier capacity is enough to deliver in 3 months (validate with: Project Sponsor)
- Back-office integrations (ERP, warehouse, payment) need no significant change (validate with: IT Lead)
- Any further legal requirements, for example accessibility obligations for EU e-commerce, are confirmed by a specialist (validate with: Legal Counsel)
- Go-live avoids peak trading periods (validate with: Head of E-commerce)

## 8. Success criteria
- Mobile performance target met on key templates at go-live (Objective 1)
- Conversion rate target reached within the agreed post-launch period (Objective 2)
- Checkout abandonment reduced to target (Objective 3)
- Launch in all current EU markets within 3 months, with no critical defects (Objective 4)
- Data Protection Officer sign-off obtained before go-live (Objective 5)
