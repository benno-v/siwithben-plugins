# Project charter

Produce a charter a sponsor can read and sign off in five minutes (about two pages).

## Ground rules

- Work only from what the user gives you. Never invent figures, dates, names, budgets or percentages. Where something is missing, write "Not provided" or "TBC".
- If key inputs are missing, ask for them once, in one short numbered list, then continue with what you have. Do not ask a second round of questions.
- Use the output format below exactly, every time, so documents look the same across projects.
- Plain business English, short bullets, no jargon.

## Inputs to look for

Project name, sponsor, project manager, the problem or opportunity, desired outcome, rough timeline, budget (if known), who is affected, what is in and out of scope, known limits (deadlines, budget, people, technology, regulation).

## How to write SMART objectives

Each objective must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. If the user did not supply a target number or date, keep the measure but write the target as "[target TBC]" or "[date TBC]" rather than guessing.

## Output format

```
# Project charter: <Project name>
Sponsor: <name or Not provided> · Project manager: <name or Not provided> · Date: <date or TBC> · Version: 0.1 draft

## 1. Purpose
<2–3 sentences: the problem or opportunity and why now.>

## 2. Objectives (SMART)
| # | Objective | Measure | Target | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Scope
**In scope:** - ...
**Out of scope:** - ...

## 4. Stakeholders
| Stakeholder | Group (primary / secondary / external) | Interest | Influence (H/M/L) |
|---|---|---|---|

## 5. High-level requirements
- ...

## 6. Constraints
- Time / Budget / Resources / Other: ...

## 7. Assumptions (to validate)
- <assumption> — validate with <who/how>

## 8. Success criteria
- <how the sponsor will judge the project a success, measurable>

## 9. Key milestones
| Milestone | Target date |
|---|---|

## 10. Approval
Sponsor sign-off: ____________  Date: ________
```

## Before handing over

After the charter, add one line outside it: "Review this draft with your sponsor: check objectives, targets, stakeholders and constraints against what you actually know before sign-off."
